# Rule interactions — independent study

These relationships come from a full-source interaction pass. The engineering labels are project-derived; they do not claim that Gal named the relationships this way.

## 1. Chancery cluster

Rules: `GAL-KANZ-VERB`, `GAL-KANZ-NOUN-CHAIN`, `GAL-KANZ-PASSIVE`, `GAL-KANZ-PSEUDOFORMAL`, `GAL-COMPOUND-FAILURE`.

Nominalization, noun chains and passive voice can be separate choices, but in the book they repeatedly combine into one failure mode: action, actor and relation disappear behind an official shell. When several appear together, local synonym replacement is usually weaker than reconstruction from the semantic skeleton.

## 2. Voice is multi-dimensional

Rules: `GAL-VOICE-PERSONA`, `GAL-VOICE-SITUATION`, `GAL-VOICE-INTERNAL`, `GAL-VOICE-AGE`, `GAL-REGISTER-ERA-CULTURE`, `GAL-EMOTIONAL-TACT`.

No single feature determines voice. A child's language depends on this child and this scene; a professional term depends on audience; cold formality may be mismatch in one scene and deliberate distancing in another.

## 3. Russian contextual economy

Rules: `GAL-EXPLICITNESS`, `GAL-SYNTAX-RUSSIAN`, `GAL-FOCUS-WORD-ORDER`, `GAL-SENTENCE-BOUNDARY`.

Do not remove repeated subjects/pronouns first and then independently randomize word order. Resolve referents, information structure and intended pause together. This interaction is especially important when source-language grammar requires explicit material that Russian can recover from morphology/context.

## 4. Image system

Rules: `GAL-LEX-COLLOCATION`, `GAL-IMAGE-COLLISION`, `GAL-IMAGE-LITERALIZATION`, `GAL-POLYSEMY-COLLISION`, `GAL-PHYSICAL-PLAUSIBILITY`, `GAL-SOUND-COLLISION`.

Accidental comedy often emerges from interactions: a normal idiom becomes literal next to a concrete image; a polysemous word awakens a second sense; a physical action becomes impossible; sound makes a serious phrase comic. These are paragraph/local-context phenomena, not token bans.

## 5. Idiom function × culture × speaker

Rules: `GAL-IDIOM-CONTAMINATION`, `GAL-IDIOM-FUNCTION`, `GAL-REGISTER-ERA-CULTURE`, `GAL-VOICE-PERSONA`.

An idiom can be correct Russian and still wrong for the world/speaker, or unfamiliar but exactly right. Translation-specific material therefore requires functional, cultural and voice checks together.

## 6. Whole before detail

Rules: `GAL-WHOLE-BEFORE-DETAIL`, `GAL-CHARACTER-CONTINUITY`, `GAL-POV-CONSISTENCY`, `GAL-SUBTEXT-RESTRAINT`, `GAL-RHYTHM-PACE`.

A local strengthening can move POV, explain subtext, make a character harsher, or change scene tempo. These rules must be evaluated at paragraph/scene/document level.

## 7. Editor workflow

Rules: `GAL-EDITOR-NOT-DICTATOR`, `GAL-EDITOR-THIRD-SOLUTION`, `GAL-SELF-EDIT`, `GAL-COMPOUND-FAILURE`.

Recommended sequence extracted from the method:

1. diagnose the actual defect;
2. distinguish defect from editor preference;
3. preserve semantic/functional invariant;
4. choose among multiple solutions instead of treating the first edit as authoritative;
5. re-read the result as a new text.

## 8. Reference verification precedes stylistic correction

Rules: `GAL-VERIFY-REFERENCE`, `GAL-LEX-PRECISION`, `GAL-WHOLE-BEFORE-DETAIL`.

If a proposed edit depends on whether an allusion, name, fact or technical term is correct, source verification comes before style optimization. Unknown-to-editor is not equivalent to wrong.

## 9. Long sentence × boundary × rhythm

Rules: `GAL-LONG-SENTENCE-CLARITY`, `GAL-SENTENCE-BOUNDARY`, `GAL-RHYTHM-PACE`.

Sentence length is not an independent objective. A long period may be clear and rhythmic; a short-fragment series may be functional or mechanical. Edit only after identifying the movement of thought.

## 10. Borrowing × term × audience

Rules: `GAL-BORROWING-FIT`, `GAL-TERM-AUDIENCE`, `GAL-REGISTER-ERA-CULTURE`.

The same borrowed word may be a neutral technical term, incomprehensible jargon, generational marker, historical anachronism or decorative pseudo-prestige. Origin alone decides nothing.

## 11. Positive repetition × anti-repetition

Rules: `GAL-RHYTHM-PACE`, `GAL-VOICE-AGE`, `GAL-EXPLICITNESS`, `GAL-SUBTEXT-RESTRAINT`.

Later positive chapters explicitly demonstrate functional lexical and syntactic repetition: child intensification, rhythmic insistence, motif/refrain. Therefore the earlier anti-heaviness observations cannot be generalized into `repetition = bad`.

## 12. Translation fidelity × Russian naturalness

Rules: `GAL-LEX-PRECISION`, `GAL-IDIOM-FUNCTION`, `GAL-SYNTAX-RUSSIAN`, `GAL-FOCUS-WORD-ORDER`, `GAL-RHYTHM-PACE`.

Word-for-word similarity is not the invariant. In translation the project should preserve meaning, image, voice, rhythm and pragmatic function; in original Russian the same source material becomes an analogy for natural rewriting, not evidence that every surface difference needs editing.

## Ordering summary

1. establish facts/referents and source certainty;
2. establish speaker, situation, genre, era/culture and POV;
3. identify the semantic skeleton;
4. solve interacting lexical/syntactic/image issues jointly;
5. check the local solution against the whole;
6. preserve author-valid unusual choices;
7. re-read the edited result.
