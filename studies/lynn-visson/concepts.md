# Concepts — Lynn Visson reverse study

## Reverse contrastive direction

The source teaches Russian speakers to avoid Russian interference in English. `humanizer_russian` reverses only those distinctions that survive the inversion:

`ENGLISH PATTERN → RUSSIAN NATIVE PATTERN → LIKELY INTERFERENCE/CALQUE → DIAGNOSIS → POSSIBLE RUSSIAN REPAIRS`.

A difference in the source does **not** imply that the inverse surface form is an error. Inversion is valid only when the source exposes a semantic role, valency, event type, reference relation, discourse function or conventional formula that Russian realizes differently.

## Event construal before morphology

English tense/aspect and Russian verbal aspect do not align one-to-one. Diagnose `state / process / boundary / result / iteration` first. This general mechanism supports the existing Russian `ломаться` state-transition model, but Visson is not cited as the source for that concrete lexical pair.

## Valency as meaning

A false friend is often not a bad dictionary word in isolation. The error can be a transferred argument structure: who asks whom **about what**, who lends **to whom**, what a verb can take as subject/object, and which complement type belongs to a sense.

## Pragmatics is not grammar

Chapters about optimism, directness, etiquette, punctuality, advice, congratulations and gestures contain useful contrastive context. Generalizations about Russians/Americans remain claims or reviewer context; they are not promoted to `NORM` or mechanical findings.

## Modern Russian overrides historical contrast

The book’s early-2000s description of a lexical contrast is evidence about that source and period, not a permanent veto. Modern dictionaries/corpus evidence can narrow or overturn the operational inference. `амбициозный проект` is the clearest case in this study.
