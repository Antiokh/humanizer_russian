# Concepts

1. **Norm is not author authority.** Orthography, punctuation and morphology from 1997 are historical evidence unless the current norm is independently confirmed.
2. **Lexical choice is semantic and stylistic.** Meaning, collocation and register outrank word-level blacklists.
3. **Functional standard vs stamp.** Official formulas can be legitimate in their native genre; leakage into another register is the editing problem.
4. **Redundancy can be expressive.** Pleonasm/tavtology is edited by semantic function, not by counting repetition.
5. **Variants have scope.** Morphological and syntactic alternatives differ by meaning, register, usage and sometimes period.
6. **Word order carries information structure.** Russian order is flexible but not arbitrary.
7. **Government is lexeme-specific.** Preposition/case choice needs the governing lexeme and current norm.
8. **Complex syntax is a resource.** Homogeneous, subordinate, participial, gerund and nominal constructions are not globally inferior to shorter paraphrases.
9. **Precision beats recall.** The book supplies many list-like prescriptions, but most are unsafe as regexes without parsing or current dictionaries.
10. **Edition lineage matters.** The 1997 source is useful as provenance and change evidence even when later Rosenthal-lineage books already encode the same mechanism more fully.
