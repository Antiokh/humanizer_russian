# Concepts — independent extraction from «Живой как жизнь»

Status: `EXTRACTED`. This file deliberately does **not** map concepts to the current `humanizer+ru` architecture. It records the source's own distinctions first.

Provenance labels follow `docs/book-study-framework.md`.

## C01 — Живой язык: изменение при наличии текущей нормы

**Source:** гл. 1–2, especially `SRC:L68-L168`, `SRC:L440-L489`  
**Basis:** `SOURCE_REPEATED`  
**Scope:** general; historical + contemporary-to-source; language system

The book rejects two symmetrical simplifications:

1. language is fixed and every novelty is corruption;
2. language changes, therefore any current form is equally acceptable.

For Chukovsky, change is continuous, but literary usage still has historically situated norms. The editor therefore needs both diachronic tolerance and synchronic discrimination.

Decision consequence: **novelty is not itself a diagnosis; change is not itself an acquittal.**

## C02 — Normalization as adoption over time

**Source:** `SRC:L286-L313`, `SRC:L468-L488`, `SRC:L1183-L1203`  
**Basis:** `SOURCE_REPEATED`  
**Scope:** word/phrase; historical; general usage

A form can move from strange → contested → common → unremarkable. The book repeatedly treats widespread, durable uptake as evidence that an innovation has become part of the language.

The concept is not reducible to popularity at one moment. Chukovsky's examples involve **time, spread, repeated use, generational replacement and loss of felt novelty**.

## C03 — «Соразмерность и сообразность»: contextual fit

**Source:** `SRC:L922-L949`, `SRC:L1195-L1210`, `SRC:L1562-L1588`, `SRC:L2762-L2774`  
**Basis:** `SOURCE_DIRECT`  
**Scope:** phrase → document; audience-specific; genre-specific; professional; familiar

The same word or construction can be appropriate in one communicative situation and bad in another. Relevant dimensions explicitly present in the source:

- who speaks;
- to whom;
- where;
- when;
- genre;
- audience education/knowledge;
- professional community;
- familiar/home situation.

This is one of the book's central decision models.

## C04 — Ellipsis vs lexical semantic change

**Source:** `SRC:L369-L379`  
**Basis:** `SOURCE_DIRECT`  
**Scope:** phrase/sentence; spoken and informal written language

The book explicitly separates:

- omission of a recoverable component (`эллипсис`);
- a word acquiring a new valency or lexical meaning, where no omitted component is actually recovered.

This is a diagnostic distinction, not a general license to delete words.

## C05 — Functional borrowing vs prestige borrowing

**Source:** гл. 3, especially `SRC:L665-L1000`; contextual summary at `SRC:L922-L949`  
**Basis:** `SOURCE_REPEATED`  
**Scope:** word/term; audience-specific; professional/domain-specific

Foreign origin is not a defect. A borrowing may:

- fill a lexical gap;
- name a technical/social concept precisely;
- be assimilated and ordinary;
- be necessary for a specialist audience;

or it may be used without need, understanding or contextual fit.

The decision variable is **function in current usage**, not genealogy alone.

---

## C06 — Compression vs opaque aggregation

**Source:** гл. 4, especially `SRC:L1122-L1203`  
**Basis:** `SOURCE_REPEATED`  
**Scope:** token/word; abbreviation; professional + public language

Abbreviation is one mechanism of linguistic economy. The source distinguishes successful compression from bureaucratic agglomeration. A form that is shorter by administrative construction can still be costly to pronounce, recognize or understand.

Operational dimensions in the source include:

- actual shortening;
- pronounceability;
- recognizability;
- established uptake;
- phonetic shape;
- register.

## C07 — Voice belongs to a speaker, role and scene

**Source:** `SRC:L1371-L1396`, `SRC:L1483-L1505`  
**Basis:** `SOURCE_DIRECT`  
**Scope:** scene; author/character; social/professional register

A lexical item that would be inappropriate in neutral narrator prose may be necessary in dialogue because it identifies a character, occupation, milieu or social relation.

The book therefore treats voice not as a global vocabulary list but as a relation between **speaker + situation + function**.

---

## C08 — Surface symptom vs extra-linguistic cause

**Source:** `SRC:L1471-L1494`  
**Basis:** `SOURCE_DIRECT`  
**Scope:** diagnostic/meta; social language phenomena

The book repeatedly warns that a visible linguistic phenomenon may be a symptom rather than its own cause. Editing a surface form cannot automatically be treated as changing the social/psychological source that produced it.

For an editor this matters chiefly as an inference boundary: **do not infer that lexical cleanup fixes the cause, or that the word itself proves the cause.**

## C09 — Канцелярит as register capture, not “official vocabulary”

**Source:** гл. 6, especially `SRC:L1562-L1588`, `SRC:L1650-L1698`, `SRC:L1880-L2008`  
**Basis:** `SOURCE_REPEATED`  
**Scope:** phrase → document; genre/register

The book explicitly defends stable official formulas in official documents and attacks their migration into contexts where they replace ordinary human naming, action and emotional speech.

Thus `канцелярит` in the source is not equivalent to `contains formal word`. Its recurring mechanisms include:

- register leakage;
- prestige inflation;
- indirect naming;
- nominalization/action burial;
- syntactic opacity;
- stock frames replacing propositions;
- repeated formulae.

---

## C10 — Prestige inflation / pseudo-scientific abstraction

**Source:** `SRC:L1650-L1716`, `SRC:L1970-L1993`  
**Basis:** `SOURCE_REPEATED`  
**Scope:** word → sentence; scientific/public/official register

A simple proposition can be wrapped in abstract or classificatory language to look more educated/scientific without gaining precision. The book's test is functional: does the complicated wording contribute a distinction, or only status and opacity?

This concept is narrower than “prefer simple words.” Legitimate technical classification remains possible.

---

## C11 — Template as repeated function, not forbidden token

**Source:** `SRC:L1744-L1785`, `SRC:L2017-L2041`  
**Basis:** `SOURCE_REPEATED`  
**Scope:** phrase → document/corpus

Almost any strong expression can become a stamp through mechanical repetition. Conversely, the source repeatedly says the individual word itself may be perfectly good.

The unit of analysis is therefore something like:

`lexical/phrase pattern × repeated discourse function × frequency/context`.

A stop-list loses this distinction.

---

## C12 — Proposition/content vs evaluative shell

**Source:** `SRC:L1738-L1756`, `SRC:L2017-L2063`, `SRC:L2088-L2103`  
**Basis:** `SOURCE_REPEATED`  
**Scope:** sentence → paragraph; analytical/expository prose

Generic interpretation can replace direct contact with the object being discussed. The book contrasts text-specific observation and individual judgment with formulae that can be written regardless of what was actually read or observed.

The operational concept is not “never evaluate”; it is **evaluation anchored in proposition/evidence rather than substituting for it**.

---

## C13 — Informational redundancy vs expressive redundancy

**Source:** `SRC:L2400-L2449`; qualification in note `SRC:L4463-L4470`  
**Basis:** `SOURCE_DIRECT`  
**Scope:** phrase/sentence; spoken/written; rhetorical

Two elements may duplicate dictionary information while jointly carrying:

- rhythm;
- duration/intensity of emotion;
- sound pattern;
- conventional formula value.

Therefore semantic redundancy and functional redundancy are not the same thing. The source also notes that an expressive formula can later become a dead bureaucratic stamp, so preservation is context-dependent.

## C14 — Sound, rhythm and articulation as constraints

**Source:** `SRC:L2400-L2472`; read-aloud examples `SRC:L1880-L1911`, `SRC:L1970-L1988`  
**Basis:** `SOURCE_REPEATED`  
**Scope:** word → sentence; prosody/phonetics

The book treats pronunciation, rhythm, repeated sounds and acoustic shape as factors in why a form succeeds or why a sentence becomes clumsy.

This is a real editorial dimension in the source, but some of Chukovsky's causal explanations (“the people selected suffix X for beauty”) are stronger than the evidence he supplies and belong in the claims audit.

## C15 — Lexicalization / semantic bleaching

**Source:** `SRC:L2473-L2582`  
**Basis:** `SOURCE_DIRECT`  
**Scope:** word/phrase; diachronic semantics

Words can lose awareness of their original image/etymology while preserving or acquiring a current conventional meaning. The book regards such forgetting as a normal prerequisite for efficient communication.

Decision consequence: literal etymological decomposition is not a valid default test of current meaning.

---

## C16 — Idiom holism

**Source:** `SRC:L2583-L2677`  
**Basis:** `SOURCE_DIRECT`  
**Scope:** phrase; phraseology

An established idiom is normally processed as a semantic whole; its internal words are not freely substitutable. Its old literal imagery may be effectively invisible in ordinary use.

This concept creates a boundary against two bad analyses:

- correcting the idiom by literal logic;
- treating its dead internal imagery as if it were a live authored metaphor.

---

## C17 — Deliberate decomposition of an idiom

**Source:** `SRC:L2605-L2677`  
**Basis:** `SOURCE_DIRECT`  
**Scope:** phrase; literary/humorous/author-specific

Writers can deliberately reactivate or deform a familiar idiom. The effect works precisely because the conventional model is recoverable.

The source explicitly distinguishes deliberate artistic deformation from accidental contamination of two expressions.

## C18 — Normative conservatism as a stabilizing force

**Source:** `SRC:L2276-L2342`  
**Basis:** `SOURCE_REPEATED` (via Peshkovsky discussion)  
**Scope:** literary language; historical/systemic

The book does not end in pure descriptivism. It presents conservatism of literary norm as one force that slows change enough to preserve continuity across generations, while criticizing uninformed purism.

The resulting model is dynamic equilibrium, not `new = good` or `old = good`.

---

## C19 — Deliberate normalization / language policy

**Source:** `SRC:L2160-L2275`  
**Basis:** `SOURCE_DIRECT` as Chukovsky's program; external validity unresolved  
**Scope:** societal/systemic; historical

Chapter 8 argues that education, mass media and organized correction can influence language practices. This is a source concept and historical program, not automatically an editorial rule for a text-processing system.

Its empirical and political assumptions require separate claims audit.

## C20 — Professional norm and familiar norm as scoped systems

**Source:** `SRC:L2759-L2777`  
**Basis:** `SOURCE_DIRECT`  
**Scope:** professional/domain-specific; familiar/home; spoken

Immediately before his prescriptive dictionary, Chukovsky gives two explicit caveats:

1. a professional community can maintain traditional forms specific to that profession;
2. familiar/home speech depends on the individual situation and cannot be normalized by simply applying the same public-formal standard.

These caveats materially limit every later dictionary pair.

## C21 — Historical prescriptive dictionary is heterogeneous evidence

**Source:** `SRC:L2759-L4146`, notes `SRC:L4147-L4530`  
**Basis:** `SOURCE_DIRECT` + structural observation  
**Scope:** historical; pronunciation, morphology, government, collocation, register

The final `Нельзя говорить / Надо говорить` table mixes several kinds of judgment:

- stress/pronunciation;
- spelling/phonological substitutions;
- inflection/morphology;
- government/preposition;
- lexical choice;
- collocational contamination;
- pleonasm/etymological arguments;
- register.

The two pre-dictionary caveats show that the list itself is not context-free even in the source. Therefore the table cannot be treated as one homogeneous kind of rule.

## C22 — Correctness is not the whole of verbal quality

**Source:** `SRC:L2135-L2159`, `SRC:L2660-L2677`  
**Basis:** `SOURCE_DIRECT`  
**Scope:** document/voice; stylistic quality

The book closes its main argument by distinguishing formal correctness from richness of vocabulary and intonation. A text may contain no obvious errors and still consist of dead templates.

This is a quality model, not a numerical metric. The source supplies no defensible scalar formula for “richness”, “culture”, “liveliness” or “humanity”.
