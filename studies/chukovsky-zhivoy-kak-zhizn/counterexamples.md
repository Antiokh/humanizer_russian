# Counterexamples and boundary cases — independent Chukovsky study

Status: `EXTRACTED`.

Every atomic rule already contains an original counterexample. This file records the **boundary mechanisms** that prevent the study from degenerating into a prohibition list, and points back to the atomic rules they constrain.

## CE01 — New can still be wrong

Constrains: R01, R02.  
Source basis: chapters 1–2 and 8.

The anti-purist argument does not imply that novelty licenses every form. The book later insists on correcting forms it regards as actual errors. Therefore `new/unfamiliar` only blocks a premature verdict; it does not decide the verdict.

Original case: a new misspelling produced by keyboard autocorrect is not protected as “language evolution”.

---

## CE02 — Old can still be current

Constrains: R01, R03, R35.

Age is not a reason to delete a form either. Long-established vocabulary and constructions may remain neutral, marked, literary, regional or professional.

Original case: an old idiom used naturally in contemporary prose should not be modernized merely because its components are historically opaque.

---

## CE03 — Apparent omission can be lexical meaning, not ellipsis

Constrains: R04, R05.  
Source basis: `SRC:L369-L379`.

The source explicitly uses the contrast between recoverable omission and a newer lexical use where no missing object is mentally supplied.

Original case: a standalone anxiety verb in current colloquial speech should not receive a fabricated object just because an older valency frame once required one.

---

## CE04 — Borrowing can be the clearest word

Constrains: R06, R07.

A specialist term known to all participants can be shorter and more precise than an explanatory Russian paraphrase.

Original case: a backend engineering team need not replace every established English-origin term with a longer schoolbook phrase.

---

## CE05 — Native-looking word can still be jargon or obscure

Constrains: R06, R07, R10.

Origin is not equivalent to accessibility. A Russian-formed internal abbreviation or occupational coinage may be less understandable than an international term.

Original case: a company-specific clipped Russian department name should be expanded for new hires even if it contains no foreign roots.

---

## CE06 — Abbreviation can reduce, not increase, effort

Constrains: R09.

Widely recognized forms are not improved by compulsory expansion.

Original case: repeatedly expanding a universally recognized technical initialism inside an expert runbook adds noise.

---

## CE07 — Slang can be accurate characterization

Constrains: R11, R12.

Neutralizing every rough/colloquial form can falsify speaker identity and scene.

Original case: dialogue from a mechanic, gamer or teenager may require their actual group vocabulary.

---

## CE08 — Slang does not prove inner character

Constrains: R12, R13.  
Source basis: chapter 5's own concession that talented, decent young people may use fashionable slang.

Original case: one `жесть` in a chat licenses a register observation, not a diagnosis of intelligence or morality.

---

## CE09 — Formal formula can be exactly right

Constrains: R14, R15.  
Source basis: `SRC:L1562-L1580`.

Stable official wording can be functional in a power of attorney, act or other formal genre.

Original case: do not conversationalize a standard legal clause merely because it looks bureaucratic.

---

## CE10 — Informal context can intentionally imitate officialese

Constrains: R15.

Register mismatch itself may be the joke or character trait.

Original case: `На основании вышеизложенного требую чай` in a comic family chat should not automatically be normalized.

---

## CE11 — Abstract classifier can carry a real taxonomy

Constrains: R16.

A generic noun is not automatically prestige inflation if the classification is meaningful.

Original case: `медицинское изделие` may encode a regulatory category that `прибор` does not.

---

## CE12 — Nominalization can be functional

Constrains: R17, R23.

Process nouns are legitimate in headings, taxonomies, compact technical statements and contexts with unknown/intentionally backgrounded agents.

Original case: `Проверка оборудования обязательна перед запуском` is not improved by inventing a checker.

---

## CE13 — Modifier can look predictable but define scope

Constrains: R18.

Deletion tests must compare reference, not only apparent redundancy.

Original case: `критические ошибки` cannot become `ошибки` when noncritical errors are intentionally excluded.

---

## CE14 — One cliché-like phrase is not a cluster

Constrains: R19.

The source repeatedly says the individual word or expression may be good. Mechanical serial use is the problem.

Original case: one precise `яркий пример` with an actual example is not itself a defect.

---

## CE15 — Cliché does not prove insincerity

Constrains: R20.  
Source basis: `SRC:L1764-L1778`.

The source's mourner can feel genuine grief yet fall into a stock formula. This is an internal counterexample against the source's own stronger rhetoric about clichés masking indifference.

Original case: a condolence formula can be conventional and sincere at the same time.

---

## CE16 — Evaluation can be the requested content

Constrains: R21, R27.

Not every evaluation needs to become an analytical proposition.

Original case: when asked `понравился ли тебе фильм?`, `Да, понравился` is complete even without evidence.

---

## CE17 — Read-aloud awkwardness can be intentional

Constrains: R22, R31.

Poetry, comic prose, sound symbolism and repeated technical terms can deliberately create marked sound.

Original case: an alliterative campaign line may preserve repeated consonants because the sound is the device.

---

## CE18 — Case-chain length is not the error

Constrains: R23.

The source gives ugly and ambiguous chains, but the operative defect is parsing/role ambiguity, not a universal integer threshold.

Original case: a technical noun phrase with several genitives may be unambiguous for its domain audience.

---

## CE19 — Metadiscourse can encode hierarchy

Constrains: R24.

Some announcing phrases genuinely change modality or navigation.

Original case: `Важно: после ротации ключа старый токен перестанет работать` is a warning, not empty self-announcement.

---

## CE20 — `вопрос` can actually mean issue/question

Constrains: R25.

Original case: `Вопрос о сроках остаётся открытым` cannot be replaced with a direct action without changing the proposition.

---

## CE21 — Shared template can be required by comparison design

Constrains: R26, R28.

Standardized matrices intentionally use the same dimensions across subjects.

Original case: compliance reports should not invent different rhetorical structures for each control merely to sound individual.

---

## CE22 — Secondary interpretation can be the task

Constrains: R27.

The source criticizes formulaic school interpretation, not the existence of criticism or scholarship itself.

Original case: a literature review can summarize established interpretations when sources are supplied and distinctions are preserved.

---

## CE23 — Lexical repetition can improve precision

Constrains: R28, R19.

Synonym rotation is dangerous where terms have fixed meanings.

Original case: an API specification should repeat `request` and `response` rather than cycle through approximate synonyms.

---

## CE24 — Fresh semantic collision is not protected by etymology arguments

Constrains: R29.

Established conventional oddities and new accidental contradictions are different mechanisms.

Original case: a newly composed administrative phrase that simultaneously asserts presence and absence should be unpacked rather than defended as “language is illogical anyway”.

---

## CE25 — Semantic redundancy can be prosodically functional

Constrains: R18, R30, R31.  
Source basis: `SRC:L2400-L2449`.

Original case: a deliberate two-part emphatic formula is kept when the second element extends the emotional beat rather than adds a fact.

---

## CE26 — Expressive repetition can later become a stamp

Constrains: R30, R19.  
Source qualification: note in `SRC:L4463-L4470`.

The same surface formula may be expressive in one occurrence and dead in another. Historical origin does not permanently protect it.

Original case: a phrase repeated in every corporate resolution can lose the emotional force that once justified its redundancy.

---

## CE27 — Heavy sound may be terminologically necessary

Constrains: R31.

Euphony cannot outrank technical identity.

Original case: a long chemical or legal term remains if replacing it makes the category less precise.

---

## CE28 — Idioms can have conventional variants

Constrains: R32.

Chukovsky sometimes states idiom invariability too absolutely. Modern phraseology explicitly studies standard variation. Therefore the safe rule is `do not freely synonymize components`, not `no idiom ever varies`.

Original case: two attested conventional variants of the same idiom can both be legitimate.

---

## CE29 — Deliberate idiom play and contamination can look identical locally

Constrains: R33, R34.

Context and recoverable effect decide.

Original case: a one-word substitution is preserved inside a sustained joke but corrected in neutral prose when it only mixes two formulas.

---

## CE30 — Current authority can agree with the historical dictionary

Constrains: R35.

Historical age blocks blind import, not use of the item after modern verification.

Original case: if a current normative dictionary independently confirms the same stress and register, it can become a current norm rule on that independent evidence.

---

## CE31 — Professional variant is not universal variant

Constrains: R36.

Original case: a stress accepted in a professional community should not be forced into general broadcast narration.

---

## CE32 — Familiar register still has constraints

Constrains: R37.

The book's phrase `не подлежит нормализации` is too strong if extracted literally. Familiar speech is situation-dependent, but ambiguity, reference failure and task-breaking errors remain possible.

Original case: a joking family message can be colloquial without making an unclear date or amount acceptable.

---

## CE33 — Anti-purism does not cancel norm

Constrains: R38.

The whole book, especially chapters 8–10, refuses the simple equation `language changes → anything goes`.

Original case: a confirmed agreement error in a text explicitly requested in standard literary Russian is corrected even if it is common in some speech communities.

---

# Boundary checklist

Before turning any surface trigger into a rule outcome, ask:

1. Is this current norm, historical change, register, audience, voice, idiom, or actual semantic error?
2. Is the pattern established or freshly produced?
3. Is repetition informationally dead or functionally expressive?
4. Does the form add technical/legal/social precision?
5. Is apparent incompleteness true ellipsis or a different lexical/syntactic construction?
6. Are we judging the text or inferring unsupported traits of its author/speaker?
7. Does the proposed correction preserve the proposition and role structure?
8. Does the source itself provide an exception to our summary?

If these questions cannot be answered, the correct outcome is an unresolved/contextual finding, not an automatic rewrite.
