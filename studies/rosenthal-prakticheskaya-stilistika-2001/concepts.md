# Concepts

1. **Functional choice precedes surface preference.** Practical stylistics is primarily a choice among variants that differ in meaning, register, expressiveness, audience fit or communicative function.
2. **Norm is a boundary, not a universal rewrite style.** A variant may be expressive or colloquial without being an error; mandatory current norm remains owned by the project `russian/NORM` layer.
3. **Synonymy is rarely absolute.** Lexical and grammatical alternatives generally preserve a shared core while changing nuance, distribution, rhythm or register.
4. **Historical source status matters.** Morphological lists and government variants in a 2001 handbook can be valuable provenance without being current prescriptions.
5. **Written style remains function-sensitive.** Scientific, official, publicistic, artistic and conversational resources can overlap; characteristic features are not hard exclusivity rules.
6. **Complex syntax is not intrinsically bad.** Word order, agreement, government, homogeneous structures, period and paragraphing are judged by clarity and function rather than length.
7. **Compression is a resource with guards.** Participles, gerunds and verbal nouns can improve density when semantic relations remain clear.
8. **Expressiveness is legitimate.** Tropes and rhetorical figures are not noise by default and should be preserved when they serve image, rhythm, contrast or characterization.
9. **Reported speech is perspective management.** Direct speech, authorial framing and free indirect speech encode different degrees of distance and voice.
10. **Unique cycle-6 mechanism: free indirect dual voice.** §105 explicitly describes a two-plan construction in which the narrator's grammatical frame carries the character's lexicon, intonation, evaluation and internal viewpoint.
